House Lights

The moment in a small theater just before the show starts: deep plum walls, one warm spotlight, a hand-lettered marquee. Your audience is improv jams, comedy nights, and standing friend groups — so the design borrows from their world: show posters, playbills, ticket stamps.

Every color on this page is extracted from the existing wordmark — the candy-striped W already contains plum #2f2440, coral #ee6c4d, cream #f8e3c9, teal #3aa38b, and amber #e9a13b. The old app used one of those five and invented a navy the logo never had. This direction uses all of them, each with a job. Zero emoji, zero glass blur, zero animated gradient sky.

Six moves, all derived — none defaulted

Palette from the wordmark

The logo is the brief. Plum #2f2440 becomes the stage (background), cream the ink, and the three candy stripes become functional color: teal = going/free, amber = pending/time, coral = action. No navy, no gradient sky.

Time wears the marquee

This product is about time, so time gets its own voice: every date, count, and status is set in Spline Sans Mono, uppercase, letterspaced, amber — a ticket stamp. Nothing else gets that treatment.

Ink-on-candy buttons

Plum text on coral, not white on gradient. It passes contrast (4.8:1 — the current white-on-coral fails at 2.9) and reads show-poster, not SaaS. AI defaults never do dark-on-accent.

The stripe is the brand thread

The W's candy stripe appears as a 3px rule in exactly three places: the hero card's top edge, the who's-in progress bar, and the lock-moment banner. It is the celebration — no confetti, no emoji.

Poster, not placeholder

Events without a photo get a typographic playbill — Fraunces at poster scale on plum with a grain texture — instead of an emoji tile on a gradient. Every event looks art-directed by default.

Paper grain, no glass

Surfaces are solid plum with a 5% grain and hairline borders. No backdrop blur, no drifting clouds, no glow. Depth comes from one shadow on one element per screen.

Palette

Five hues from the logo trace plus two derived neutrals. Contrast ratios validated against WCAG — every pairing listed here passes for its role.
